# BROKENNESS
## Pieces of Me — Session 1: Sin

**Session Objective:** Understand how the first fracture occurs. Where sin originates, how it works its way in, the slow fade that desensitizes us to it, the difference between *struggling* with sin and *sinning*, and what guilt and conscience are actually doing inside us.

---

## OPENING

**Icebreaker:** Think of something that once bothered your conscience and doesn't anymore — a show, a joke, a habit, a way of talking. You don't have to share what it is. Just notice: *when* did it stop bothering you?

**Opening prayer:** *"Search me, God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts. See if there is any offensive way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting."* (Psalm 139:23–24)

---

## PART 1 — WHAT IS SIN? (A Working Definition)

Before we talk about how sin develops, we need to agree on what it actually is.

> "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." — Romans 3:23

> "Everyone who sins breaks the law; in fact, sin is lawlessness." — 1 John 3:4

> "If anyone, then, knows the good they ought to do and doesn't do it, it is sin for them." — James 4:17

Put simply: **sin is missing the mark of who God made us to be, and it shows up two ways — doing what God says not to do, and failing to do what God says to do.** It's not just an act. It's a broken relationship with God expressed through action, thought, or neglect.

**Questions:**
1. Before tonight, how would you have defined sin in your own words?
2. James 4:17 includes *failing to act* as sin, not just wrong action. Does that change how you think about your own life?

---

## PART 2 — WHERE DID SIN ORIGINATE?

Sin isn't something humanity invented from nothing. Genesis shows us exactly where the fracture began.

**Read Genesis 3:1–6 aloud.**

Notice the pattern — it's the same one we'll see repeated in David, Samson, and Solomon:

1. **The serpent questions God's word** — *"Did God really say...?"* (v.1) — doubt planted first.
2. **The serpent distorts God's word** — exaggerating the restriction, then flatly denying the consequence (v.4).
3. **Eve evaluates the fruit** — *"good for food... pleasing to the eye... desirable for gaining wisdom"* (v.6) — desire builds.
4. **She takes and eats. She gives to Adam, who was with her, and he eats too** (v.6).

The very first sin already contains the whole pattern this study is about: a thought entertained, a word twisted, a desire that grows, and then the act — which was never really the beginning, just the visible end.

> "Therefore, just as sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin, and in this way death came to all people, because all sinned." — Romans 5:12

**Questions:**
1. What's the first move the serpent makes — is it an action, or a question?
2. Where in your own life has a doubt about what God actually said been the opening move toward compromise?

---

## PART 3 — HOW DOES SIN ACTUALLY HAPPEN?

James gives us the mechanism in miniature — the same pattern from Eden, now internalized in every human heart.

> "Each person is tempted when they are dragged away by their own evil desire and enticed. Then, after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin, and sin, when it is full-grown, gives birth to death." — James 1:14–15

Temptation itself isn't sin — Jesus was tempted in every way, yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15). Sin begins when desire is **entertained** rather than resisted.

**Question:** What's the difference between a thought that knocks and a thought we let in and feed?

## PART 4 — THE SLOW FADE: HOW WE BECOME DESENSITIZED

This is where Psalm 1 becomes so important — it doesn't just describe an act, it describes a *drift*.

> "Blessed is the one who does not **walk** in step with the wicked or **stand** in the way that sinners take or **sit** in the company of mockers." — Psalm 1:1

Notice the progression in the Hebrew imagery:

- **Walk** — casually associating, just passing through, "I'm just looking / just listening / just curious."
- **Stand** — pausing, lingering, staying in the environment longer than you meant to.
- **Sit** — settling in, becoming comfortable, identifying with what once felt foreign.

This is desensitization in three verbs. Nobody sits down with sin on day one. They walk past it, then they stand near it, then one day they realize they've been sitting there for a while and it doesn't feel wrong anymore.

**Culture's role in the fade:** A lot of what shapes what feels "normal" to us isn't a conscious decision — it's repeated exposure. Music that makes betrayal sound romantic. Shows that make deception look clever instead of destructive. Jokes that turn something Scripture calls serious into something to laugh at. None of it asks for a decision. It just asks for repetition — and repetition is exactly how a conscience gets recalibrated.

> "See to it, brothers and sisters, that none of you has a sinful, unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God. But encourage one another daily, as long as it is called 'Today,' so that none of you may be hardened by sin's deceitfulness." — Hebrews 3:13

**Questions:**
1. Think of something culturally common — a genre of music, a type of show, a kind of humor — that you were once uncomfortable with and now aren't. What changed: your convictions, or your exposure?
2. Where are you currently "walking" near something that could become "standing," or "standing" near something that could become "sitting"?
3. What's one thing you've stopped noticing because you've been sitting in it too long?

## PART 5 — HOW ONE COMPROMISE LEADS TO ANOTHER

**David — the fall before the fall (2 Samuel 11:2–5).** He *saw*, then *asked*, then *sent*, then *took*. Every small decision made the next one easier. The visible sin was only the final expression of an invisible progression.

**Samson — repeated compromise until numbness set in.** He kept playing near boundaries he knew he shouldn't. Eventually, *"he did not know that the LORD had left him"* (Judges 16:20) — he'd grown so used to compromise, he couldn't tell how far he'd drifted.

**Solomon — the slow, almost invisible drift.** *"As Solomon grew old, his wives turned his heart after other gods"* (1 Kings 11:4). No single dramatic decision — just years of small ones.

**Question:** Which of these three — escalation (David), numbness (Samson), or drift (Solomon) — most describes a pattern in your own life right now?

## PART 6 — STRUGGLING WITH SIN VS. SINNING

This distinction matters, and Psalm 1 draws it clearly: there's a real difference between a temptation you're *fighting* and a lifestyle you've *settled into*.

> "Blessed is the one who does not walk... stand... sit..." — Psalm 1:1

The blessed person in this Psalm isn't someone who never feels pulled toward sin — the Psalm wouldn't need to describe resisting *walking, standing, and sitting* if the pull wasn't real. The blessing is for the one who **doesn't stay** — who feels the tug and keeps moving instead of settling in.

Paul describes the same tension from the inside:

> "I do not understand what I do. For what I want to do I do not do, but what I hate I do... For I have the desire to do what is good, but I cannot carry it out." — Romans 7:15, 18

That's the voice of someone *struggling* — in the fight, hating the sin even while feeling its pull. That's different from someone who has stopped fighting altogether and calls the sin part of their identity.

> "If we claim to be without sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness." — 1 John 1:8–9

**Questions:**
1. Where in your life are you currently *struggling* with something (still fighting, still convicted) versus somewhere you may have quietly *settled* (stopped fighting, made peace with it)?
2. What does 1 John 1:9 promise to someone who's honest about either?

## PART 7 — WHAT IS GUILT? WHAT IS CONSCIENCE?

**Conscience** is the internal alarm God built into every person — the capacity to sense right and wrong even apart from Scripture.

> "...they show that the requirements of the law are written on their hearts, their consciences also bearing witness, and their thoughts sometimes accusing them and at other times even defending them." — Romans 2:15

**Guilt** is what that alarm produces when it goes off — the internal signal that something is wrong. Guilt itself isn't the enemy; it's meant to be a warning light, not a life sentence.

But an alarm can be damaged by repeated overriding:

> "...through the hypocrisy of liars whose consciences have been seared as with a hot iron." — 1 Timothy 4:2

A "seared" conscience doesn't mean the person feels *no* guilt because they're innocent — it means the alarm has been silenced by repetition. This is the endpoint of the slow fade in Part 4: walk, stand, sit, and eventually the alarm stops sounding altogether.

There's also a difference in what guilt *does* to a person:

> "Godly sorrow brings repentance that leads to salvation and leaves no regret, but worldly sorrow brings death." — 2 Corinthians 7:10

- **Godly sorrow (true conviction)** moves you toward God — confession, change, freedom.
- **Worldly sorrow (shame)** moves you away from God — hiding, self-punishment, despair, or numbing the guilt instead of dealing with its source.

**Questions:**
1. Has your conscience ever gone quiet about something it used to flag? What happened in between?
2. When guilt shows up in your life, does it tend to drive you toward God (confession, change) or away from Him (hiding, shame, avoidance)?

---

## PART 8 — SYMPTOMS OF A HEART DRIFTING

Pulling the threads together, here's what the "slow fade" tends to look like from the inside, before the visible act:

- A boundary that used to feel firm now feels flexible.
- Content, humor, or company that once made you uncomfortable no longer does.
- You've started explaining or defending a choice you used to just avoid.
- Conviction shows up less often, or more quietly, than it used to.
- You've stopped bringing a certain area of your life into the light with anyone.

**Question:** Which of these, if any, do you recognize in yourself right now?

---

## PART 9 — THE WORD'S RESPONSE

Scripture doesn't tell us to wait until sin becomes a habit — it tells us to deal with it early:

> "Flee from sexual immorality." — 1 Corinthians 6:18
> "Resist the devil, and he will flee from you." — James 4:7
> "Make no provision for the flesh." — Romans 13:14
> "Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it." — Proverbs 4:23

And when we haven't caught it early:

> "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness." — 1 John 1:9
> "Therefore, confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed." — James 5:16

## BRING IT HOME — Closing Discussion

1. Where does my compromise usually begin — in a thought, a conversation, a piece of content, a small choice?
2. What have I normalized that once convicted me?
3. Am I currently walking, standing, or sitting in an area I know I shouldn't be?
4. Is what I'm feeling right now conviction (pointing me to God) or shame (pushing me away from Him)?
5. Who can I bring this into the light with this week, so it doesn't stay invisible?

**Closing prayer:** Ask God to reveal one specific place where you've been sitting instead of walking away — and to give you the courage to bring it into the light rather than manage it alone.
