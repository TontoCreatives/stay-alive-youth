When the Ground Moves: Finding an Anchor in Matthew 24

If you look at the news, scan social media, or even just pay attention to your own local community, it’s easy to feel like the world is constantly on the edge of a breakdown.

Political tension, economic crises, betrayal, and relentless noise. In times like these, our default setting as human beings is panic. We search for stability in our bank accounts, our leaders, our plans, and our routines. But what happens when the very things we built our security on begin to shake?

Jesus didn't leave His followers in the dark about this. In Matthew 24, as He walked out of the temple in Jerusalem, His disciples did what most of us do: they pointed out the grand architecture around them. They looked at the massive stone buildings of the temple—the ultimate symbol of religious, cultural, and national stability—and admired its strength.

Jesus looked at those massive stones and dropped an unsettling truth:

"Do you see all these things?" he asked. "Truly I tell you, not one stone here will be left on another; every one will be thrown down." — Matthew 24:2

Imagine the shock in that moment. What do you do when Jesus tells you that the most permanent-looking structure in your world is about to fall?

The Trap of the Deceiver and the Sound of Alarm

Naturally, the disciples pulled Him aside later on the Mount of Olives and asked two things: When will this happen? And what will be the sign of Your coming?

Pay attention to the very first thing Jesus said in response. He didn't start with a timeline or a series of end-times predictions. He started with a warning about their internal state:

"Watch out that no one deceives you." — Matthew 24:4

When the world gets unstable, our vulnerability goes through the roof. People start selling quick fixes, false hopes, and sensational predictions. Jesus warned that many would come in His name saying, "I am the Messiah," and mislead many.

Then He described what the atmosphere of the world would look like: wars, rumors of wars, nations rising against nations, famines, and earthquakes.

For many of us, reading that list triggers immediate anxiety. But look at how Jesus commands us to respond to global chaos:

"See to it that you are not alarmed. Such things must happen, but the end is still to come." — Matthew 24:6

He calls these things "the beginning of birth pains." Birth pains are painful, intense, and uncomfortable, but they aren't the end of the story—they are the indication that something new is being brought forth.

When the Shake Gets Personal

It’s one thing when nations fight; it’s another thing when the shaking hits your personal life.

From verse 9 onwards, the passage moves from global events to intimate, personal pressures:

  • Persecution and hatred: Being targeted or sidelined for holding to the truth.
  • Betrayal: People you trusted turning on you ("many will turn away from the faith and will betray and hate each other").
  • Cold hearts: "Because of the increase of wickedness, the love of most will grow cold."

That last line is perhaps the most dangerous threat in a shaking world. When you face repeated disappointment, betrayal, and systemic injustice, the natural defense mechanism is to build a wall around your heart. You stop trusting, you stop caring, and your love grows cold.

Systemic chaos doesn't just destroy buildings; it threatens to harden the human soul.

The Two Anchors That Stand

So how do we remain standing when everything around us—and within us—is being tested? Jesus gives two clear instructions in verses 13 and 14:

1. Endure to the End

"But the one who stands firm to the end will be saved." — Matthew 24:13

Standing firm isn't about having all the answers or never feeling afraid. It’s about refusing to abandon your post. It’s choosing not to let your love grow cold even when wickedness increases. It’s staying rooted in Christ when your circumstances are shifting like sand.

2. Stay Driven by the Real Mission

"And this gospel of the kingdom will be preached in the whole world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come." — Matthew 24:14

Unstable times have a way of narrowing our vision until we only care about self-preservation. But Jesus redirects our focus: chaos isn't a signal to retreat and hide; it’s the backdrop against which the Gospel of the Kingdom shines brightest.

The Kingdom of God is not built on human institutions, political stability, or personal comfort. It is an unshakeable reality. When human systems fracture, it simply exposes how desperately the world needs the hope that doesn't crumble.

Questions to Sit With This Week

1. Where Is Your Anchor? When news or life circumstances shake your peace, what is the first thing you run to for security (finances, control, distraction, or God's Word)?
2. Checking the Temperature: Has the hostility, betrayal, or pressure of recent seasons caused your love for God or people to grow cold? How can you guard your heart against bitterness?
3. Standing Firm: What is one area of your spiritual life where you feel tempted to give up right now, and what does "standing firm" look like practically in that situation this week?
