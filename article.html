Theological Reflection August 13, 2026

What Is Purpose, Really? Beyond the System, the Career, and the Unknown

When you hear the word purpose, what comes to mind? For most of us, it boils down to one simple word: Why. Why am I here? What is my objective in this life?

If you grew up like me, living in Kenya, you probably went through a very systematic way of living. You go to primary school, navigate puberty, and transition to high school. In high school, we were all supposed to figure out what we wanted to do with our lives. But if I’m being completely honest, I never really understood why I was studying some of those subjects. It felt like the primary reason we were studying was just to pass an exam—an exam designed to propel us to a destination none of us were entirely certain of.

Most of us didn't actually know what we wanted to do. We just flowed with the system. For others, the harsh realities of financial struggles and life’s uncertainties made it feel impossible to even promise oneself a next step.

Beyond all the academic pressure, no one seemed to answer the deeper question: Why are we here on earth, and does God actually have something specific in store for us to accomplish?

We might have known objectively which career we wanted to pursue, the kind of person we dreamed of marrying, where we wanted to live, or how many kids we wanted to have. But were those things actual purpose, or were they just tiny fragments of a much bigger picture?

The Witnesses Before Us

When we open Scripture and study the lives of believers before us, almost every single one had something specific to accomplish—either directly or indirectly. Even the quietest servants had a role.

These people were deeply flawed. They made mistakes, and some even wandered off track, yet God was still glorified through them, and His plan prevailed.

Consider Paul. Near the end of his life, writing from a cold Roman prison cell, he penned these iconic words:

"I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith." — 2 Timothy 4:7

Did Paul actually finish what God called him to do? Absolutely.

Look at Joseph, who was betrayed by his brothers and sold into slavery, only to preserve a nation during a famine. Look at Moses, stuttering through his insecurities at the burning bush, called to confront Pharaoh. Look at David, a forgotten shepherd boy anointed to be king, or Samson, whose life was messy and compromised, yet God used him to deliver His people.

How did they live out their purpose? How are we supposed to live out ours? And more fundamentally: How do we even find it?

To answer that, we have to re-examine what "purpose" actually means.

Defining Purpose: Dictionary vs. Scripture

To unpack this, we need to contrast how the world defines purpose with how God reveals it.

1. The Dictionary View (Human-Centric)

The dictionary defines purpose as:

"The reason for which something is done or created, or for which something exists; an intended result, aim, or goal."

In the worldly sense, purpose is often treated as a target you set for yourself. It’s tied to ambition, career milestones, personal fulfillment, and legacy. Under this definition, you are the creator, and you decide your own reason for existence.

This is why so many people feel lost after achieving their goals. You can reach the top of your career, build the house, start the family, and still wake up asking: "Is this it?"

2. The Biblical View (Creator-Centric)

Scripture flips the entire equation. Purpose is not something you invent; it is something you discover.

"For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them." — Ephesians 2:10

In the biblical framework:

  • God is the Manufacturer: A product does not define its own purpose; the maker does. A chair doesn't decide to be a car.
  • Purpose Existed Before You Did: God didn't create you and then try to figure out what to do with you. He designed specific "good works" beforehand and then formed you specifically to walk in them.
  • Purpose is Ultimate, Calling is Contextual: Your ultimate purpose never changes—it is to know God and bring Him glory. Your calling or assignment (your job, your relationships, your season) is simply the terrain where that purpose gets lived out.

How Do We Live It Out?

Living out your purpose isn't about having a 20-year blueprint laid out before you. Joseph didn't know the prison would lead to the palace. David didn't know taking bread to his brothers would lead to Goliath.

They simply did two things:

  • Faithfulness in the Present Assignment: Purpose is lived in the now, not just in some distant future. If you are a student, a designer, a teacher, or a friend right now, God’s purpose for you today is obedience and excellence in this space.
  • Surrender Over Ambition: Samson’s life shows us that personal ambition can derail us, but surrender aligns us. Living out purpose requires yielding your plans to God's authority.

When you stop viewing purpose as a destination you have to build on your own, and start viewing it as a walk of obedience with the One who designed you, the anxiety fades.

You don't have to figure out every step of the next ten years. You just have to trust the God who already prepared the path, and walk faithfully in the chapter He has you in today.

Questions for Personal Reflection

Before you move on to the rest of your day, take a few minutes to sit with these:

1. The School/Career Trap: Are you currently pursuing a goal because it aligns with who God made you to be, or simply because it’s the next expected step in the "system"?
2. Fragments vs. Whole: Looking at your life right now—your job, your relationships, your hobbies—are you treating those as your entire purpose, or as the terrain where God wants you to serve Him?
3. The Present Assignment: If God doesn't give you a 10-year blueprint today, what is the single assignment of obedience He is asking you to be faithful with right now, in this season?
4. Surrender Over Ambition: What is one personal plan or outcome you need to stop forcing so you can let God dictate the direction?
